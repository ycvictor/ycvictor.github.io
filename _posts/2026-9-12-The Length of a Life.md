# The Length of a Life

Recently, some of my friends who have started working have told me that they’ve noticed themselves taking far fewer photos.

Maybe it’s because work has made them busier. Or maybe it’s because work has gradually become a part of everyday life. When we spend our days doing similar things over and over again, there simply aren’t as many moments that feel worth capturing.

That made me think back to the times when I was traveling alone, or experiencing the World Cup for the first time in my life. I took so, so many photos during those periods.

Photos are a way of indirectly documenting our lives. In a way, they also reflect how much we were experiencing during a certain period of time.

We often see people talking about how to live healthier and how to extend our healthy years. But if we live longer while every day looks almost the same, would that life really be richer? Would it really feel longer?

If we imagine life as a photo album, and ourselves as the camera, perhaps all those highly similar everyday photos would eventually be reduced to just a few.

Maybe ordinary days are recorded at 30 fps. But when something truly meaningful happens, we unconsciously switch to 120 fps. The details become sharper. Every second feels clearer.

Perhaps the true length of a life has never been measured simply by the days on a calendar. Maybe it is measured by how many moments you have that are worth remembering.

So perhaps we should bring a little more surprise into our lives from time to time, and make the ordinary days worth remembering. And perhaps we can do the same for the people around us.

The best gift you can give someone isn't more years. It's more to remember.

And for ourselves, perhaps we should live fully, intensely, and wholeheartedly. Because the more deeply we experience something, the more deeply it stays with us.

As Avicii once said: One day you'll leave this world behind, so live a life you will remember.
